Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3) is an object storage service: you put files into it,
each with a name, and get them back from anywhere with a web request. It's one of the oldest and
most widely used services in AWS, and a building block behind data lakes, websites, backups and
mobile apps.

This journey explains Amazon S3 from the ground up, one short step at a time. You don't need an
AWS account to follow along. Each step links to the AWS documentation it's based on.

Across six steps you'll go from *what Amazon S3 is* and *how buckets and objects fit together*,
through *storage classes*, *security and access*, and *versioning and lifecycle rules*, to *how
Amazon S3 compares with Amazon EBS and Amazon EFS*. Interactive diagrams, a storage-class picker
and a sorting exercise let you try each idea as you read.
